Not an expert on mindfulness, especially in an org context. Most of what I’ve read about it has focused on the benefits, like [being less likely to avoid uncomfortable information](https://blog-about-people-analytics.netlify.app/posts/2023-06-05-mindfulness-and-objectivity/). So this particular finding caught me by surprise.

In a randomized field experiment, employees high in Machiavellianism who received mindfulness training reported more contempt, anger, and disgust toward coworkers than those in either control group (regulated breathing or a waitlist). Those feelings were also linked to more self-reported workplace incivility.

A little reminder that what helps some people can backfire for others. Also a good selling point for estimating heterogeneous treatment effects. The average can hide who responds differently.

And a separate point about having a testable theory of how things might work. Of all the things you could look at here, would Machiavellianism have come to mind? Kay’s theory gives a concrete reason to test it. His idea is that paying closer attention to what you’re feeling can bring existing contempt for coworkers into sharper focus.

Link to the paper 👉 [The Dark Side of Mindfulness at Work](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10551-026-06513-3).
